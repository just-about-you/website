Features

What the app actually does.

Everything on this page ships today. Nothing here is planned, in beta, or described from a design document — if it is written below, you can open the app and use it.

Recording things

Track the two things you care about, or all of them.

Day-to-day readings

Sleep, blood pressure, blood sugar, blood oxygen, heart rate, water, and trackers you make up yourself. Each gets one wedge on the home wheel: tap, type, done.

Hold the plus or minus and it speeds up, so entering a blood pressure is not forty taps.

Trends, including the empty ones

Every metric you track appears here whether or not it has any data yet — an entry point that vanishes when you have not used it is a feature you forget you have.

Colours against a reading come from published general guidelines, not from anything personal to you.

Things that are not numbers

A symptom journal, a cycle calendar and a weight trend each get their own page rather than a wedge, because "logged today" is not a useful thing to say about them.

Medications

A list with daily doses to tick off. If you share the category, a carer sees whether you took them — and, only if you tag them, which ones.

Choose what you track

Turn off everything you do not use. The wheel, the trends list and the widget all follow, so an unused metric stops taking up room.

People

Nobody can find you. You hand out an invite, or nothing happens.

There are no usernames, no directory and no search. If you have not given someone an invite yourself, there is no way for them to reach you — not by guessing, and not by looking you up.

Friends, and a daily "I'm all right"

Add someone with a single-use invite you send them however you like. Check In tells the people you chose that you are fine today. It carries a date and nothing else.

Names live on your phone, not ours — you label people yourself, and that label never leaves the device.

The care circle

Someone who looks after you sees whether you logged a category today. Not the number. If they need an actual figure they have to ask for that one reading, and it is sent only if you approve that request.

Turning a category off removes its past days too — what they can see is always what you allow now, not what you once allowed.

Shared tasks

A task you and a friend can both see and tick off — a repeat prescription to collect, an appointment to get to. The words are encrypted on your phone to the people on the task, so the server stores text it cannot read.

On the device

The bits that matter when the phone is out of your hands.

App Lock

Your fingerprint, your face, or a PIN in front of the whole app. Off by default — it is offered during setup, and declining costs you nothing.

Backup and restore

An encrypted file that you keep, wherever you like. Only your password opens it. See Help for the part that cannot be recovered.

Home-screen widget

One metric per page with a small chart, turning over on its own without waking the app. Choose which metrics appear.

Reading feed

Short articles on sleep, stress and the rest, fetched in a way that is not tied to you — the request carries no identity at all.

Themes

Several palettes, light and dark. The app is readable at large text sizes; screens are tested at double the normal size.

Delete everything

One button empties the database, clears your settings, cancels scheduled reminders and overwrites the freed space so the old rows are not left readable on disk.
