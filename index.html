Three things worth knowing

No account, ever

No email address, no phone number, no password. Your phone generates a random code the first time you open the app, and that code is the only thing that identifies you to us.

The numbers stay here

Every reading you enter is written to an encrypted database on this phone. There is no column for a blood pressure or a blood sugar anywhere on our servers, so there is nothing there to leak.

Sharing is a yes or a no

If someone looks after you, their app shows whether you logged a category today — not what you logged. They can ask you for one reading, and it is sent only if you approve that request.

The part most apps leave out

What a stolen database would contain

We think you should be able to check this rather than trust it. If our entire database were taken tomorrow, it would hold no name, no email address, no phone number, no postal address, no date of birth, no password, and no health reading.

It would hold random codes, the links between them, the dates people checked in, a yes-or-no per category per day, and text encrypted on the phones that wrote it. Nothing in it names, contacts, or locates a person.

This is not medical care

Your Health is a self-help tool for recording how you feel and tracking things over time. It is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any illness, and it is not a substitute for professional advice. Colours shown against readings come from published general guidelines, not from anything personal to you. If a result concerns you, contact a doctor.
