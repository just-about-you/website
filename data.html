Data

Every place anything is kept.

Not a summary — the actual list. Seven places hold something, and the section after them is what is provably not in any of the ones we run.

The seven places

On your phone, encrypted

Every reading, note, medication, task and setting. The database is encrypted with a key held in the phone’s own secure storage, which is the same place your other apps keep their credentials.

Sixteen tables. This is the copy that matters — it is the one that survives us.

On our server, durable

Twenty tables, none of which has a column for a number you recorded. What is there: random codes that stand for people, which of those codes are linked to which, whether a category was logged on a date, the encrypted text of anything you chose to share, the public half of each phone’s key, a delivery token for notifications, and a 30-day log of which code called what.

We can see that two codes are connected. We cannot see who they are, or what either of them recorded.

On our server, briefly

Anything one phone is handing another: a reading you approved sending, or the medications you tagged for a carer. Encrypted to their phone and deleted when they pick it up, with an expiry even if they never do.

None of it is written to the durable side. A reading is held in memory on the receiving phone and written nowhere; a tagged medication list is kept, which is the seventh place below.

In the phone’s secure storage

The key to your database, this device’s keypair, and the token that proves your phone owns its random code. Nothing you typed.

In ordinary settings

Preferences: which metrics you track, your theme, what you have already seen. Every key is classified, and the ones that would be meaningless or unsafe on another phone are left out of a backup.

In files

Only what you asked for: a backup you exported, wherever you chose to put it. We never see it, and cannot open it.

On a carer’s phone, if you tagged medications

The names, doses and week of adherence you tagged for them, in their own encrypted file rather than in their records. It is the one place where something of yours sits on somebody else’s device.

It is left out of their backup on purpose, it is wiped if either of you ends the link, and it goes when their app does. It exists because our copy is deleted on pickup, so theirs is the only one left.

What is provably not there

“Provably” is doing real work in that heading. Each of these is a column that does not exist, which is a stronger thing than a policy that says we would not look.

No reading, value or number

No blood pressure, blood sugar, oxygen, heart rate, weight, water, sleep length, cycle day or symptom severity appears as a column anywhere on our side.

The sharing table holds a yes-or-no named logged, and nothing else.

No name of any kind

Not your real one, and not a chosen one. No table has a name column and no route reads or writes one.

What you call the people you know is held on your phone and is never transmitted at all.

No email, phone number or password

There is no account to have them in. Your phone generates a random code on first run and that is what we know you by.

How this list stays true

It does not, on its own. This page is written by hand against the table definitions, and in August 2026 an audit found it two counts and two places out of date — nineteen tables where there were twenty, six places where there were seven. Saying it was generated when it was not is how it drifted for that long.

What does hold it now is narrower and real: the sentences this page must not contain are checked before it is published, so the absolutes it used to carry cannot come back. The tests behind the underlying claims are listed on the checking page, and they guard the app rather than these words.
