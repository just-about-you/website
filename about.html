About

Why this exists.

Most health apps want an account before they will hold a single number, then keep those numbers on a server for as long as the company lasts. This one was built the other way round: your readings live on your phone, and the parts that genuinely need a server were designed to carry as little as they possibly could.

The idea

Two reasonable people, pulling in opposite directions.

Somebody tracking their blood pressure usually has a reason, and often has somebody who worries about them. Those two facts pull against each other. The person who worries wants to know you are all right. You may not want to hand over a running commentary on your body to get them to stop asking.

The compromise this app makes is a yes-or-no. A carer sees that you logged today, which answers the question they actually have. If they want a number, they have to ask for it, and you decide each time — including deciding no, which tells them nothing except that you said no. Medications are the one thing that works differently: you tag them for a carer one at a time, and a tag stands until you take it off rather than being asked again each time.

That is a small idea. It is also the whole product, and most of the engineering exists to keep it honest.

A carer's real question is "are they all right?" — not "what was it this morning?"

How we talk about privacy

Written to be checked, not believed.

We name the exceptions

The privacy page lists what is stored, what leaves the phone, and what a stolen database would contain — including the places where the strong version of the claim stops being true. You should hear those from us rather than find them yourself.

No absolutes we cannot keep

This site used to say readings "never leave the phone". That was too strong: one reading can cross, encrypted, when you approve a specific request for it. The sentence was wrong, so it was changed rather than defended.

Claims are tied to tests

The important ones are held up by tests that fail the build if the behaviour drifts — including one that searches a produced backup file, byte by byte, for data that should not be in it.

What we store What it does

This is not medical care

Your Health is a self-help tool for recording how you feel and keeping track of things over time. It is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any illness, and it is not a substitute for professional advice. If a result worries you, speak to a doctor.

Who builds this

One person, independently

Not a company with a funding round to answer to, and not a team whose priorities can change with an acquisition. That is worth knowing in both directions: it is why the data model can stay this strict, and it is why there is no support desk.

No investors, no advertising

Nobody is paying to be shown to you, and nothing about you is for sale. There is no third-party analytics and no tracking library in the app at all.

Your records outlive us

They are on your phone in a file you can export. If this project stops tomorrow, nothing of yours goes with it — which is a deliberate property, not a happy accident.

What this will not do

A short list, and a firm one. Each of these has been considered and turned down rather than simply not built yet.

Tell you what a reading means about you

Blood pressure, blood sugar and oxygen are coloured against published general guidelines — the same ones a leaflet would cite. Nothing in the app goes further and states a verdict, and the code refuses the words that would: normal, high, low, stage, overweight, obese. Interpreting your number is a conversation with a doctor.

Ask you for an account

No email, no phone number, no password. Every feature that looks like it needs one has been built so that it does not.

Let anyone look you up

There is no directory and no search. Sharing means making a single-use invite. A device code you handed out before invites existed still works if someone pastes it, which is deliberate — breaking an old message would be a worse answer than honouring it.

Hold a name for you

Not a real one and not a chosen one. What you call the people you know stays on your phone.
