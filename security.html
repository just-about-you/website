Security

What we did, and what we got wrong.

A security page that only lists reassurances is a marketing page. This one says what an audit found, what changed, and what we know is still imperfect.

The shape of it

What you share is encrypted before it goes

The database on your phone is encrypted. The words of a shared task, and a reading you approve sending, are encrypted on your phone with a key sealed to the person receiving them.

That is the content. The connection itself is a separate question, and the honest answer is below.

Every request proves who is asking

Each phone holds a token that proves it owns its own random code, and every route that touches a person compares the two. Tokens can be revoked, and deleting your data revokes yours.

There is no password to steal

No account means no credential database, no reset flow to abuse, and nothing to reuse from a breach somewhere else.

What an audit found

An internal security review in August 2026 found real problems. Naming them is more useful than claiming there were none.

Anyone could act as anyone

There was no check that a request came from the phone it claimed to. Every route that names a person now proves it first.

A code could be used to take an account

Registering a code that already existed could overwrite it. Doing that now requires the token belonging to it.

Text was written into a page unescaped

A hole in the operator dashboard, closed by building the page properly rather than by filtering what goes into it.

Things we know are imperfect

The connection is not encrypted yet

The app talks to our server over plain HTTP, not HTTPS. Anyone positioned between your phone and us can see which random code is making a request, which categories you have shared, and the token that proves your phone owns that code.

This is the largest finding in our own audit and it is not fixed. What it does not expose: a reading, a medication name, or the words of a shared task, because those are encrypted before they are sent. What it does expose is enough, and a captured token is good for months.

This site loads fonts from Google

Two typefaces come from a third party, which means your browser tells them you opened this page. Nothing else here calls anyone. We think that is worth saying rather than leaving you to find it.

Some claims have no test

Three of them, listed by name on the checking page rather than left for you to notice.

A lost backup password cannot be recovered

By anyone, including us. That is the design working, and it is also the sharpest edge in the app.

Reporting something

If you find something that affects other people’s data, please tell us before you publish it. A route for that is being set up alongside the business address — see contact.
