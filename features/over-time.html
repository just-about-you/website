Over time

A run of readings, side by side.

One reading says very little. The point of writing them down is the shape they make over weeks.

All features

Where a run of readings lives

On Home, and then on each number’s own screen. There used to be one screen called Trends holding all of them at once, and there is not any more.

Home gives every number you track its own place. The ones that make a chart get a tile with the latest value; symptoms and periods, which are not numbers, get a button. Tap either and it opens that thing’s own screen, with the chart and the detail underneath.

Every tracked number gets one

What appears is exactly what you chose to track, and nothing decides otherwise on the way. A number you have never recorded still gets a tile, showing that there is nothing in it.

Why an empty tile is not a mistake

If a tile vanished when it had no data, you would have no way to tell “I have not recorded this” from “this is not something I track”. Those are different, and only one of them is worth acting on.

So the tile stays, empty, until you fill it. The same rule survived the screen it was written for: it now decides what Home shows, and a test fails the build if a tracked number ever loses its place.

Looking further back

Day, week, month or year

Four buttons under the row of days change how much of your history the chart is showing. This is per number, on that number’s own screen — there is no one screen that charts all of them together.

Look up an older day

The strip of days above the chart moves it through the last week. For anything older, the calendar button opens a date picker and lists every reading recorded on the day you pick, which is where you go to correct one.

Touch the chart to read one day

Tap or drag along the chart and it reads out the value for the day under your finger. Each day is also its own step for a screen reader, so the same run of values can be heard without a gesture.

Choose what appears

What you track is a setting, in the menu under What you track. Turn something on and it gets a place on Home; turn it off and past entries are kept.

Everything here runs on your own phone

No account, no email address, and no reading of yours in our database. You can check that rather than take our word for it.
