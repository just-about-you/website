Accessibility

What is tested, and what falls short.

Most accessibility statements describe an intention. This one lists what is asserted by a test that would fail, and names the one thing we know does not meet the bar.

In the app, asserted

Text at 200% on a small screen

Every walkthrough step is checked at double text size on a 360 pixel screen. Nothing may truncate or overflow.

Reduce motion means no motion

Turn it on and the walkthroughs give the same steps and the same words with nothing moving — not a shortened animation, none.

A screen reader gets the whole step

Each step announces as one piece rather than as fragments that can be interrupted, and in the order it is meant to be heard.

Where it falls short

One known failure, recorded rather than quietly carried.

One theme’s main button is below the bar

In the original colour family, the primary button’s text sits at 3.32 against its background where the standard asks for 4.5.

It is not fixed rather than not noticed: darkening it enough drops the button’s own contrast against the page below the bar for a control, so it needs a per-brightness colour rather than a nudge. Every other family passes.

On this site

Checked before anything is published

Colour contrast and a 360 pixel layout are verified by a script that runs before the site goes anywhere, along with a skip link, a page language and alt text on every image.

Tell us if we have missed something

An accessibility problem is a bug, and we would rather hear about it than have you work around it. See contact.
