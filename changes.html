Releases

What’s new.

Rendered from the changelog that ships with the app, so the words here are the release notes rather than a retelling of them. It is regenerated by hand, not on publish, so it can lag a release — in August 2026 it lagged two. It cannot say something the changelog does not.

Each entry describes the release it belongs to, and later work can overtake one without coming back to correct it. Two examples as of August 2026: the app is now at 2.10, and the versions after 2.4.0 — which rebuilt the themes into fifteen palettes with no separate brightness switch, and restructured the menu — have no entry here yet, because they have none in the changelog either. The colourless maximum-contrast theme that 2.4.0 mentions was removed in that rebuild. Where this page and the accessibility page disagree, the accessibility page describes today.

2.4.0 (2026-08-28)

The interface again — this time how it looks, and help that finally describes the app you are using.

Glass

  • Cards float over a soft coloured ground instead of sitting flat on paper, with a lit top edge and a shadow tinted to match. Depth comes from layering, not from borders.
  • Grayscale is unchanged. It exists for people who need maximum contrast, and translucency spends contrast, so it keeps its solid cards — and gains a crisper outline than it had before.
  • Every text and accent pair was re-checked against the colour you actually see now that cards are see-through. Seven accents were nudged deeper as a result.

Help that matches the app

  • Nine walkthroughs rewritten against the screens that ship: recording a reading, medications, adding one, checking in, friends and caregivers, caregiver access, the welcome, appearance, and Home.
  • A new walkthrough for getting around the five places, including why Care is dimmed.
  • Two things the help used to say were wrong before this release, and are now right: that a caregiver can never see a reading (they can, once, if you are asked and say yes), and that the app asks every time (medications you tag are sent without asking again).
  • The pictures in the walkthroughs still show the previous layout.

Smaller

  • Recording a reading now shows up on that metric's screen straight away. Previously the screen kept showing "no data" until you left and came back.
  • Turning medication sharing off now removes those medications from a caregiver's phone the next time their app connects, rather than only stopping new ones.
  • Trends is gone. Everything it showed lives on Home and on each metric's own screen.

2.3.0 (2026-08-28)

The interface, rebuilt. A round of testing with people outside the team came back with one near-unanimous verdict: the app was hard to make sense of. This release is the answer to that.

Home is tiles now

  • The metric wheel, the hero page and the card deck are gone. Home is a greeting, a week of days you can move through, and a tile for each thing you track.
  • A tile shows the reading for the day you are looking at, or says plainly there is no record. Nothing you track can disappear from Home because it happens to have no data.
  • Tapping a tile opens that reading over a day, a week or a month, with a Record a reading button of its own.
  • Friends and Help sit beside the greeting.

Five places instead of a scrolling stack

  • Home, Tasks, Medication, Actions and Care. Every destination is labelled, and Care announces itself as unavailable rather than sitting there doing nothing.
  • Medication is its own place: a week of days over your doses grouped by time, each with an icon for its form and one tap to record it.
  • Actions gathers checking in and sending a message.
  • Care is its own screen rather than a tab hidden inside Trends.

Two themes, and colour that can be read

  • Calm, a soft pastel set, is the new default; Loud is its bright, high-contrast counterpart. Grayscale stays for anyone who needs maximum contrast, as do the invite-only styles.
  • Every text and accent pair was checked against WCAG AA in both light and dark, and several long-standing combinations that fell just short were fixed.

Saying what is true

  • A day with no record is never drawn, spoken or counted as a missed dose. The medication week now says which it is out loud, where before a screen reader heard the same thing either way.
  • Readings are described against your own history — "higher than usual for you" — never as a verdict.

2.1.1 (2026-08-25)

Help that describes the app we actually ship.

Eleven walkthroughs, animated, in one place

  • Every major flow now has a step-by-step walkthrough with its own animated illustration: getting around Home, recording a reading, reading your trends, checking in, keeping track of tasks, friends and caregivers, inviting someone, accepting an invite, giving a caregiver access, managing medications, and backing up and restoring.
  • The first time you reach a screen, its walkthroughs play. Skip moves on to the next one rather than leaving, so nothing in a screen goes unexplained.
  • Help now lives in exactly two places — the Home screen and the main menu. The old per-screen help buttons are gone.
  • Open any walkthrough on its own from the help menu, and it returns you there when you finish.
  • The illustrations respect your reduce-motion setting: the same steps, the same words, no movement. Everything is readable by a screen reader and holds at 200% text size.

The help that was there before was out of date

  • The old guided tours covered four screens. Nothing explained People, caregivers, medications, backup, invites or the home-screen widget.
  • Those tours could only point at one thing on one screen, which is why they never covered anything that spans two.
  • Four places still told you to add people with a code. Invites replaced code-sharing some time ago; the People screen, the caregiver list, the remove-friend confirmation and the onboarding text had not caught up. All four now describe invites. Pasting a code you were given earlier still works.

Medications (shipped under 2.1.0, recorded here)

  • The medications wheel became a list grouped by the time each dose is due, with anything untimed under Daily, and a countdown to your next dose.
  • The wheel could not render real medication names. Twelve medications at four times a day is around forty-eight labels on a circle, and it was reviewed against short placeholders.
  • The list sits where the wheel did, on the Home deck, and the medications card scrolls to it rather than opening a separate screen.

2.1.0 (2026-08-23)

The release that made the privacy claims true. Most of what changed is invisible in the interface and load-bearing in what leaves your phone.

Nothing on our servers has your name any more

  • Names live on your device. The server's name columns are gone. Each phone labels its own contacts, and the label you give someone never leaves it.
  • Push notifications carry no name. The receiving phone composes "<label> says: …" from the label it already holds, so a name no longer passes through Google on its way to you.
  • The app no longer sends a display name at registration. Nothing stored it and nothing read it; it was crossing the network for nothing.
  • The display-name prompt used to say *"Friends see it, and it reaches our servers so they can find you."* Neither half was true any more. It now says what actually happens.

Shared task text is encrypted before it leaves

  • A task's words are sealed on your phone under a per-task key, wrapped to each person on the task. The server stores text it cannot read.
  • Someone with no published key gets a clear refusal instead of a task they would silently be unable to open.

Sharing a reading, one request at a time

  • A carer can ask you for a single reading. It is sent only if you approve that request, encrypted to their phone, and deleted when they pick it up.
  • Approving once does not approve the next one.
  • Medication names are shared per carer, and only while the medications category itself is switched on — the category switch is the master one and the finer consents narrow within it.
  • Your own identity key is published on registration, so sharing works on a fresh install instead of failing silently.

Fixes found on real devices

  • Marking a dose taken now reaches your carers, instead of waiting for an app restart.
  • Tagging a medication actually sends it.
  • Medications live in one place, with the sharing link where you would look for it.
  • A sharing switch that fails says so, and says why, instead of failing quietly.
  • Nothing due is no longer counted as a missed medication.
  • A new account is no longer shown as having missed a month of check-ins.
  • Four notification types were being sent at a priority Android drops while the app is backgrounded — including the prompt asking you to approve a reading request, which expired unanswered. All of them are now delivered.
  • Pull-to-refresh works on the screens that were advertising it and not doing it.

Copy that had drifted from the code

Several privacy statements were true when written and had quietly stopped being true. The onboarding privacy page, the caregiver help screen and the shared policy text now describe what the app does today, and tests hold them there.
