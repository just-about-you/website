Releases

What’s new.

Rendered from the changelog that ships with the app, so the words here are the release notes rather than a retelling of them. It is regenerated by hand, not on publish, so it can lag a release — in August 2026 it lagged two. It cannot say something the changelog does not.

Each entry describes the release it belongs to, and later work can overtake one without coming back to correct it. Two examples as of August 2026: the app is now at 2.10, and the versions after 2.4.0 — which rebuilt the themes into fifteen palettes with no separate brightness switch, and restructured the menu — have no entry here yet, because they have none in the changelog either. The colourless maximum-contrast theme that 2.4.0 mentions was removed in that rebuild. Where this page and the accessibility page disagree, the accessibility page describes today.

2.12.0 (2026-08-30)

The top of the screen was the part that had not been rethought. Now it has.

One header instead of four loose pieces

  • The greeting, Friends and Help, and the row of days now sit together in a single band that curves closed at the bottom. Before, they were four things stacked with gaps between them and nothing saying they belonged together.
  • The greeting leads with your name rather than with the time. The clock is gone — your phone already shows it, a few millimetres higher — and the date moved down to the row of days, which is where a date belongs.
  • The menu button moved up beside your name, which frees the whole bar it used to sit in. The header takes less room on a phone than what it replaced.

The row of days says which month it is

  • It never did. There is now a month above it, and two arrows for moving between weeks. You could always swipe it, but nothing said so — and swiping is not something everyone can do.
  • Every day is now a full-size target. It had been slightly too small on this screen, in a way nothing was checking.

Two things that were quietly wrong

  • With only one of Symptoms or Cycle turned on — which is how the app starts — the button sat beside an empty half-width gap. It now fills the row.
  • "Friends" and "Symptoms" were being cut short, and not only at large text sizes: they were clipped at the normal size too. Buttons now move onto their own rows rather than trimming their labels.

2.11.0 (2026-08-29)

The look the last release described is now the look on the screen.

Depth, pattern and colour that were specified but never drawn

  • Cards were meant to sit on the page with a soft shadow, a lit top edge and a gentle wash behind them. None of it was being drawn — every value was zeroed before it reached the screen, on all fifteen themes. It draws now.
  • Each theme was meant to carry its own background pattern. Every theme drew the same one. There are eighteen now, and they differ by theme.
  • The main button in each theme was meant to take that theme's colour. Six of the eight places it appears were ignoring it.
  • Seven of the light themes had the same white background as each other — the largest thing on the screen, identical across all of them. Each has its own now, from a bold olive through to two different near-whites.

A sixteenth theme, for contrast

  • Stark: black, white and grey, and nothing else. It is for reading the screen easily rather than for how it looks, and it was missing since the themes were rebuilt. Every other theme is checked against it for being distinct; this one is deliberately exempt, because being plain is the point.

Saying less when it knows less

  • The sentence under the greeting could report a settled, confident day after reading almost nothing — a long stretch of history with only a handful of entries in it counted as a full one. It now says it is still learning until it has actually seen fourteen readings, and it counts readings rather than days.

Things the app was saying that were not true

  • The app said the Caregiver Circle was paid. Nothing in the app is paid, and it now says so.
  • The first-run walkthrough introduced a Trends screen. That screen was removed two releases ago; the card now describes where the same thing actually lives, which is Home.

2.10.0 (2026-08-29)

The theme now reaches the screens it had been skipping.

The whole app, one theme

  • Open a metric from Home and it kept its own idea of what colour that metric is, unrelated to the tile you tapped and unrelated to your theme. A metric's colour on Home and its colour on its own screen were never the same. They come from one place now, for every metric and every theme.
  • Those screens also painted a fixed sand background under whatever theme you had chosen. The background, the day/week/month buttons, the reading itself and the labels around it are all yours now.
  • Charts drew their gridlines, ticks and average line in colours named outright rather than taken from the theme, so on a tinted card they simply vanished. They are also drawn on the card now rather than on the background behind it, because on a near-black ground a mark that thin has nowhere to show.
  • The marker on the week strip that says a day was logged takes the theme's second colour, which until now was authored by every theme and used by nothing.

Menus that stop drawing lines under nothing

  • Every row drew its own divider and every group boundary drew a second one, so a boundary was an indented line, a gap, a full-width line, another gap. The last row of every list also drew a line under nothing at all. There is one line between two things, and no line after the last one.
  • "What you track" moved to the top level of the menu. It was filed inside a group about how the app works, which is documentation, and it is not — it is the screen where you choose what the app tracks, and people were hunting for it.

2.9.0 (2026-08-29)

Fifteen themes, and every one of them looks like itself.

The roster

  • Twenty themes were removed and ten new ones written. What ships is Acid and fourteen others authored from colours chosen for them: Harbour, Naples, Rosewood, Carnival, Bracken, Tobacco, Slate, Meadow, Azure and the rest.
  • Calm, Loud, Grayscale, Royal Blue and Founder are gone. Whichever of them you were using, you are moved to Acid, the new default.
  • Every theme is free. The two that were invite-only no longer exist, and nothing in the app is behind a grant any more.
  • Every pair of themes was measured against every other pair for whether a person could tell two screens apart. All hundred and five pairs clear the floor; the closest two are still comfortably distinguishable. Four of the fifty supplied colours do not appear anywhere, each for a measured reason — three sit in a band where no text can be read on them, and one is too close to the card behind it.

Three things that were quietly wrong before this

  • Harbour was drawing a lavender navigation bar on a cyan theme. Mixing towards a white or near-grey colour lost the hue entirely and landed somewhere unrelated. Tobacco, Slate and Meadow had it too.
  • The colour reserved for an alarm was being adjusted as though nothing would ever be written on it. It carries text, and now it is chosen accordingly.
  • A pure white card cannot carry the soft background figure that cards draw, because there is nothing lighter than white to draw it in.

2.8.0 (2026-08-28)

A theme is a whole palette now, and choosing one is the only choice you make.

The light/dark switch is gone

  • There is no longer a System / Light / Dark control, and the setting no longer exists. Each theme is either bright or dark by its own nature, and the picker tells you which — the label is worked out from the theme itself rather than written down beside it, so it cannot disagree with what you see.
  • Changing your phone between light and dark mode no longer changes the app. It used to switch you to a different palette without asking.
  • Twenty palettes became twenty themes, and which of each pair survived was decided one at a time. Grayscale kept its bright form because that form has more contrast, and it exists for contrast. Royal Blue and Founder kept their dark forms, because what they were described as when they were offered was "black, gold and silver" and "deep emerald and bronze".
  • Four new themes: Harbour, Naples, Rosewood and Carnival.
  • If you restore an old backup, the light/dark preference it contains is dropped on the way in rather than written to a device with nothing to read it.

2.7.0 (2026-08-28)

Fifteen themes shipped in 2.6.0. This is the release that found out they all looked the same, and fixed it.

The finding

  • The same screen was rendered under every theme and the pictures compared, the way a person compares them rather than the way a test reads a colour value. A hundred and fifty-two theme pairs out of three hundred and eighty came out as the same screen — two of them closer together than a human eye can separate. Not one of the older palettes failed; it was every theme built on the shared shell, which is what the shell turned out to guarantee.

A theme is a template, not one colour

  • A theme used to be a fixed frame with a single accent dropped into it. A theme is now three to six colours of its own and a table saying where each one goes. Cards cycle through the theme's own set, so which colour a metric has still means something within a theme, and stops pretending to identify one of eleven.
  • Acid ships — a near-black green ground, a lime accent and an emerald second, sampled from the reference rather than eyeballed. Text on the accent resolves to the ground colour, never to white.

Choosing a theme

  • The theme picker was previewing colours that eight themes shared. Two of its four swatches were identical across every new theme and the other two differed by a shade, so in bright mode eight of the twenty themes drew strips that were the same image, in three groups. The accent goes first now, and the difference between the worst-matched pair went from nothing at all to plainly visible.

Smaller

  • The developer menu shared one "working" flag between six buttons, so starting one made another say it was busy. It also described a sweep narrower than the one it performs, and did not mention that a typed server address cannot connect unless it was allowed for at build time.

2.6.0 (2026-08-28)

Fifteen themes, and the end of the glass.

No glow

  • The glass look introduced in 2.4.0 is retired. Cards cast no shadow and carry no lit top edge; they separate from the background by being a step apart from it and by an outline that is actually there. The shadow was costing the most on exactly the themes with the least room to render it.
  • Cards belong to their theme. One set of card colours for the whole app meant sleep was the same lavender everywhere. Each theme works out its own set now, leaning each one slightly towards its accent — which also fixed a case where a card came out brighter than everything except the accent itself.
  • Fifteen themes, all with a bright and a dark form, none invite-only.
  • Six colours in the new set were wrong and were corrected before shipping rather than after. One target was missed and is reported rather than quietly excused: Ember's bright form has nowhere further to put its alarm colour.

2.5.0 (2026-08-28)

Three things found on a real device.

Fixed

  • Dark backgrounds were banding. A shadow dark enough to see against a near-black ground travels only a handful of steps across the height of a card, which the eye reads as visible stripes. Shadows are now worked out from how much room the background actually has, and below a certain point no shadow is drawn at all — which also stopped one theme paying for a shadow that could never have shown.
  • The accent colour had nowhere to go. Founder shipped its bright accent in the palette and on no pixel of the app. The accent now colours the selected tab, the selected day in both calendars, and the primary buttons. This turned up an older fault along the way: on every dark theme, the highlight behind the selected tab was a near-black shape on a dark bar — invisible.
  • Cards were shouting over the accent. On the accent themes they are muted now, with the difference between them carried by lightness rather than by saturation, so they stay apart from each other without competing.
  • Six of the trend arrows had lost their colour and were drawn in the wrong one.

2.4.4 (2026-08-28)

The menu, and the first of the new palettes.

Two groups instead of ten rows

  • The main menu is Help and Support, Settings, and nothing else (plus developer tools on a debug build). The three groups that used to nest inside it are dissolved rather than folded deeper — nesting would have put Appearance four taps away, and this way nothing is more than three.
  • "Display & Text Size" no longer exists as a screen. Its contents sit directly under Settings.
  • Medications and Friends left the menu. Both already have a place of their own on the bottom bar and on Home, so the menu was offering a second door to something you can already see.
  • Your device code is shown for support, with wording that says plainly it is not how you add people.

Fixed

  • The theme picker was showing colours no theme had. Its swatches were written out by hand and had drifted: Calm previewed a near-white lavender when the theme is periwinkle, and it was wrong for four of the five themes. The swatches are read from the theme itself now.

Founder

  • A new dark palette: a flat near-black ground, a lime accent and an emerald second, taken from a reference image rather than guessed. Added alongside the existing themes rather than replacing any of them.

2.4.3 (2026-08-28)

Fixed

  • Three descriptions in the developer menu were wrong, one of them in the dangerous direction — it claimed clearing demo data would leave demo medications behind and that a backup would carry them. Neither was true.

2.4.2 (2026-08-28)

Demo data you can actually look at, and a safety hole in how it was stored.

Demo data

  • Turning on demo data now fills ninety days across everything the app tracks — readings, medications, tasks, three full cycles and four custom trackers. Gaps are real gaps, so the screens show what a real history looks like rather than a solid wall of numbers.

Fixed, and this one matters

  • Demo medications were being stored as real ones. They went into your backup as real history, and on a phone with medication sharing switched on they were sent to your carers, where they were indistinguishable from your actual list.
  • Removing demo data deleted real medications. It worked by name, against a list of fifteen common ones, with no confirmation — so on a real phone it destroyed those medications and their history and reported success.
  • Tasks, task history and custom trackers had no way of recording that a row was demo data at all. They do now, so removing demo data removes demo data and nothing else.

2.4.1 (2026-08-28)

The first install of 2.4.0 on a phone. Six things were wrong and are fixed.

Fixed

  • A metric's own screen was one enormous button and no data. The record button was taking the whole height of the screen. The same fault was on the custom-tracker screen.
  • Choosing a day in the past and then recording wrote the reading to today. Readings are always recorded for today, so on any other day the record button is replaced by a line saying so and a way back to today. There is nothing left to tap that could write the wrong day.
  • One reading now draws a chart. With a single point the chart painted one rectangle from edge to edge with no scale at all.
  • "No record" stopped shouting. It was set in the same large serif as a real reading, so a fresh install was a wall of it. It takes a quieter face now.
  • An empty tile said "No record" twice, once in the value and again directly underneath it.
  • Blood pressure was rendering as "120…" — the tile gave the reading a third of the width it needs and cut the second number off. A reading that does not fit is scaled down instead.
  • Pull-to-refresh is gone from Home. It advertised a refresh it did not perform; Home updates itself when you save a reading.

Cards that tell you which metric they are

  • Every metric has its own colour and keeps it, so "sleep is the indigo one" is worth learning. The colours are spaced by how different they look rather than by where they sit on a colour wheel.
  • Colour never becomes a verdict. A card's colour is chosen from the name of the metric and from nothing else — no reading, no threshold, no date can reach it, and there is no red in the set.
  • A large soft figure sits behind each card, always shaded away from the text crossing it so the text gains contrast rather than losing it.
  • Trend arrows appear on "higher" and "lower" only, in the metric's own colour. Nothing is red or green: the shape says the direction and the colour says only which metric.
  • Symptoms and cycle left the tile grid and sit above it as buttons. They are journals, not a series of numbers, and a tile is the wrong shape for them.
  • Tiles in a row are all the same height. A tall card used to stretch an empty neighbour into a tall blank box.

2.4.0 (2026-08-28)

The interface again — this time how it looks, and help that finally describes the app you are using.

Glass

  • Cards float over a soft coloured ground instead of sitting flat on paper, with a lit top edge and a shadow tinted to match. Depth comes from layering, not from borders.
  • Grayscale is unchanged. It exists for people who need maximum contrast, and translucency spends contrast, so it keeps its solid cards — and gains a crisper outline than it had before.
  • Every text and accent pair was re-checked against the colour you actually see now that cards are see-through. Seven accents were nudged deeper as a result.

Help that matches the app

  • Nine walkthroughs rewritten against the screens that ship: recording a reading, medications, adding one, checking in, friends and caregivers, caregiver access, the welcome, appearance, and Home.
  • A new walkthrough for getting around the five places, including why Care is dimmed.
  • Two things the help used to say were wrong before this release, and are now right: that a caregiver can never see a reading (they can, once, if you are asked and say yes), and that the app asks every time (medications you tag are sent without asking again).
  • The pictures in the walkthroughs still show the previous layout.

Smaller

  • Recording a reading now shows up on that metric's screen straight away. Previously the screen kept showing "no data" until you left and came back.
  • Turning medication sharing off now removes those medications from a caregiver's phone the next time their app connects, rather than only stopping new ones.
  • Trends is gone. Everything it showed lives on Home and on each metric's own screen.

2.3.0 (2026-08-28)

The interface, rebuilt. A round of testing with people outside the team came back with one near-unanimous verdict: the app was hard to make sense of. This release is the answer to that.

Home is tiles now

  • The metric wheel, the hero page and the card deck are gone. Home is a greeting, a week of days you can move through, and a tile for each thing you track.
  • A tile shows the reading for the day you are looking at, or says plainly there is no record. Nothing you track can disappear from Home because it happens to have no data.
  • Tapping a tile opens that reading over a day, a week or a month, with a Record a reading button of its own.
  • Friends and Help sit beside the greeting.

Five places instead of a scrolling stack

  • Home, Tasks, Medication, Actions and Care. Every destination is labelled, and Care announces itself as unavailable rather than sitting there doing nothing.
  • Medication is its own place: a week of days over your doses grouped by time, each with an icon for its form and one tap to record it.
  • Actions gathers checking in and sending a message.
  • Care is its own screen rather than a tab hidden inside Trends.

Two themes, and colour that can be read

  • Calm, a soft pastel set, is the new default; Loud is its bright, high-contrast counterpart. Grayscale stays for anyone who needs maximum contrast, as do the invite-only styles.
  • Every text and accent pair was checked against WCAG AA in both light and dark, and several long-standing combinations that fell just short were fixed.

Saying what is true

  • A day with no record is never drawn, spoken or counted as a missed dose. The medication week now says which it is out loud, where before a screen reader heard the same thing either way.
  • Readings are described against your own history — "higher than usual for you" — never as a verdict.

2.1.1 (2026-08-25)

Help that describes the app we actually ship.

Eleven walkthroughs, animated, in one place

  • Every major flow now has a step-by-step walkthrough with its own animated illustration: getting around Home, recording a reading, reading your trends, checking in, keeping track of tasks, friends and caregivers, inviting someone, accepting an invite, giving a caregiver access, managing medications, and backing up and restoring.
  • The first time you reach a screen, its walkthroughs play. Skip moves on to the next one rather than leaving, so nothing in a screen goes unexplained.
  • Help now lives in exactly two places — the Home screen and the main menu. The old per-screen help buttons are gone.
  • Open any walkthrough on its own from the help menu, and it returns you there when you finish.
  • The illustrations respect your reduce-motion setting: the same steps, the same words, no movement. Everything is readable by a screen reader and holds at 200% text size.

The help that was there before was out of date

  • The old guided tours covered four screens. Nothing explained People, caregivers, medications, backup, invites or the home-screen widget.
  • Those tours could only point at one thing on one screen, which is why they never covered anything that spans two.
  • Four places still told you to add people with a code. Invites replaced code-sharing some time ago; the People screen, the caregiver list, the remove-friend confirmation and the onboarding text had not caught up. All four now describe invites. Pasting a code you were given earlier still works.

Medications (shipped under 2.1.0, recorded here)

  • The medications wheel became a list grouped by the time each dose is due, with anything untimed under Daily, and a countdown to your next dose.
  • The wheel could not render real medication names. Twelve medications at four times a day is around forty-eight labels on a circle, and it was reviewed against short placeholders.
  • The list sits where the wheel did, on the Home deck, and the medications card scrolls to it rather than opening a separate screen.

2.1.0 (2026-08-23)

The release that made the privacy claims true. Most of what changed is invisible in the interface and load-bearing in what leaves your phone.

Nothing on our servers has your name any more

  • Names live on your device. The server's name columns are gone. Each phone labels its own contacts, and the label you give someone never leaves it.
  • Push notifications carry no name. The receiving phone composes "<label> says: …" from the label it already holds, so a name no longer passes through Google on its way to you.
  • The app no longer sends a display name at registration. Nothing stored it and nothing read it; it was crossing the network for nothing.
  • The display-name prompt used to say *"Friends see it, and it reaches our servers so they can find you."* Neither half was true any more. It now says what actually happens.

Shared task text is encrypted before it leaves

  • A task's words are sealed on your phone under a per-task key, wrapped to each person on the task. The server stores text it cannot read.
  • Someone with no published key gets a clear refusal instead of a task they would silently be unable to open.

Sharing a reading, one request at a time

  • A carer can ask you for a single reading. It is sent only if you approve that request, encrypted to their phone, and deleted when they pick it up.
  • Approving once does not approve the next one.
  • Medication names are shared per carer, and only while the medications category itself is switched on — the category switch is the master one and the finer consents narrow within it.
  • Your own identity key is published on registration, so sharing works on a fresh install instead of failing silently.

Fixes found on real devices

  • Marking a dose taken now reaches your carers, instead of waiting for an app restart.
  • Tagging a medication actually sends it.
  • Medications live in one place, with the sharing link where you would look for it.
  • A sharing switch that fails says so, and says why, instead of failing quietly.
  • Nothing due is no longer counted as a missed medication.
  • A new account is no longer shown as having missed a month of check-ins.
  • Four notification types were being sent at a priority Android drops while the app is backgrounded — including the prompt asking you to approve a reading request, which expired unanswered. All of them are now delivered.
  • Pull-to-refresh works on the screens that were advertising it and not doing it.

Copy that had drifted from the code

Several privacy statements were true when written and had quietly stopped being true. The onboarding privacy page, the caregiver help screen and the shared policy text now describe what the app does today, and tests hold them there.
