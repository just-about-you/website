Privacy

What we store, and what we don't.

Most privacy policies are written to be defensible. This one is written to be checked. Everything below states what the app actually does, and names the part of it that does so.

The short version

A reading has no column here

Blood pressure, blood sugar, oxygen, heart rate, sleep, weight, water, cycle, symptoms — none of it has a column on our servers. It lives in an encrypted database on your phone. Two things can carry more than that, and both need you to act first: they are in the table below.

We hold no name

Not your real one and not a chosen one. What you call the people you know is stored on your phone and never sent to us.

There is no account

No email, no phone number, no password, no date of birth. Your phone makes a random code and that is what we know you by.

What does leave your phone

Most of this is a server passing something between you and someone you chose. The last two rows are not: they are things we send for our own reasons rather than yours, and they are here because a list that left them out would not be a list. Every request the app makes is one of the rows below.

The question worth asking

If our database were stolen

It would contain no name, no email address, no phone number, no postal address, no date of birth, no password, and no health reading. Every person in it appears as a random code their own device generated, which is not derived from anything about them.

Nothing in it names or locates a person. One thing in it can reach one: the notification delivery token, which is why it has its own card below rather than a footnote.

Where that claim stops

"Nothing identifies you" would be a stronger sentence and a false one. Three things below are worth knowing, and you should hear them from us rather than find them later.

The code is still about you

It is random and it is not your name — but it is the same code every day, so it singles you out. Regulators treat that as personal data, and so do we. It is pseudonymous, not anonymous.

The notification token

To reach your phone with a notification, we hold a delivery token issued by Google. We cannot turn it into a person. Google could. It is the strongest identifier we hold.

A daily yes or no adds up

"Logged medications" every day for a month says something about someone, even with no name attached. It is the largest thing we hold, and it exists because being able to show it to someone who cares for you is the point of that feature.

What you control

  • Sharing is off until you switch it on, one category at a time.
  • Switching a category off deletes the days it already recorded — not just future ones.
  • A carer seeing you have logged something never entitles them to the number. Each reading is asked for, and refused or approved, one at a time.
  • Medications are the one thing that works differently. You tag them one at a time, for one carer at a time, and it starts off — but a tag stands until you remove it, rather than being asked again each time. Switching medications off as a category stops all of it at once.
  • "Delete all data" clears your rows on our side and the app's data on this phone.
  • A backup file you export is the copy you can make of your own records. It is not quite the only copy off this phone: a carer you tagged medications for holds those names on their phone, in their own encrypted file, which is wiped if the link ends.

How to check any of this

Each claim above corresponds to something specific in the system. This is the map.

Claim on this page What makes it true
No reading is stored on our servers No table has a column for one. A guard test fails the build if a forbidden column name appears in the schema or a query.
We hold no name The name columns were removed from the database, and the app no longer sends one at all.
Shared task text is unreadable to us It is encrypted on the phone with a key sealed to each person on the task. A test seals with the real cipher and proves the text cannot be recovered without the key.
A received reading is never written down The code that holds it cannot reach the database, the backup or the disk cache — a test fails if those imports ever appear.
Switching a category off removes its past days History is filtered by present visibility, and the rows are deleted rather than hidden.
